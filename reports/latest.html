Executive summary
The week's dominant signal is Guangtai's five-year SATS tender win in Singapore, which converts the Chinese OEM from price challenger into a credentialed APAC handler supplier with an electrification narrative, and it is reinforced by Guangtai's deliberate positioning for electric GSE demand and a heavy international trade-show programme. In parallel, Japan and Korea are entering major fleet and structural transitions — JAL and ANA adopting the E190-E2, JAL/Toyota Level 4 autonomous towing at Haneda, and the Korean Air-Asiana integration on 17 December 2026 — each of which resets GSE specifications and procurement structures. Southeast Asia shows concrete near-term demand: Bangkok Airways' ATR deliveries from Q4 2026, Garuda's recapitalisation, Lion Air's modernisation, and Indonesia's financed 14-airport modernisation programme. The competitive threat is no longer hypothetical: TLD faces simultaneous erosion of its electric premium in Singapore and Southeast Asia and potential exclusion from Japanese automation standards. We must defend the SATS-adjacent account base immediately, secure specification influence in Japan's autonomy programme, and position leasing and aftermarket capability as the differentiators Chinese new-unit pricing cannot match.
