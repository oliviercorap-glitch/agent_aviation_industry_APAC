EU pushes Britain to raise tariffs on Chinese vehicles

Reading
Brussels has urged UK Prime Minister Andy Burnham to increase tariffs on Chinese-made vehicles, extending the tariff wall that already covers EVs and is now broadening across vehicle categories. While this concerns road vehicles rather than GSE, it signals a hardening Western trade posture toward Chinese industrial exports, which historically precedes similar scrutiny of Chinese machinery including GSE.
Business impact
If tariff escalation spreads to industrial and airport equipment, Chinese GSE exporters (Guangtai, CIMC Tianda) would face higher landed costs in Western markets, potentially slowing their price-led expansion into APAC tenders where they currently undercut TLD by 20-30%. Conversely, a bifurcated trade environment could push Chinese manufacturers to dump excess capacity into unregulated APAC markets, intensifying price competition in Indonesia, Vietnam and the Philippines.
Recommended action
Have trade-policy team model a scenario where Chinese GSE faces 15-25% tariffs in OECD markets and assess whether redirected Chinese capacity would hit TLD's APAC pricing within 2-3 quarters.
